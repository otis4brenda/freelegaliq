A first-offense DUI typically costs $10,000–$25,000 total over 3 years when every expense is counted — fines, attorney fees, DUI school, ignition interlock device, and the auto insurance hit. For most people, the insurance premium increase is the largest single cost, adding $4,000–$12,000+ over 3 years and arriving as a shock at the first policy renewal after conviction. High-BAC, repeat, or accident cases routinely push total costs to $30,000–$75,000.

Why DUIs Cost So Much More Than the Fine

Most people arrested for DUI focus on the court fine — typically $500–$2,000 for a standard first offense. That's just the entry fee. State-mandated DUI education or alcohol treatment programs add $400–$1,500. An ignition interlock device (IID) — now required in all 50 states for at least some DUI offenders — costs $80–$150/month in monitoring fees after a $100–$200 installation charge, typically required for 6–18 months. License reinstatement fees of $150–$500 are unavoidable. Then your auto insurer learns of the conviction via the DMV — in most states a DUI triggers an SR-22 filing requirement and a premium increase of 70–130%, sustained for 3–7 years depending on the state. In California, which looks back 10 years for insurance rating, the insurance surcharge alone can exceed $10,000.

Should I Hire a DUI Attorney?

For a first-offense DUI with no accident or injury, hiring an experienced DUI defense attorney typically costs $1,500–$5,000 and is almost always worth the investment. A skilled attorney can file motions to suppress evidence from an unlawful stop or improper BAC testing procedure, negotiate reduced charges (such as "wet reckless" in California), or identify procedural errors that may lead to dismissal. Successfully avoiding a DUI conviction — or reducing to a lesser charge — can save $15,000–$20,000 in downstream fines, program costs, IID fees, and insurance surcharges over several years. For repeat offenses, high-BAC cases, or accidents with injury, the stakes are high enough that experienced legal representation is essential.

Frequently Asked Questions

A first-offense DUI with a .08–.14 BAC typically costs $10,000–$25,000 all-in over 3 years. This includes: court fines ($500–$2,000), attorney fees ($1,500–$5,000 for a simple case), DUI/alcohol education program ($400–$1,500), ignition interlock device ($800–$2,000), towing and impound ($200–$500), license reinstatement ($150–$500), and the auto insurance premium increase ($4,000–$12,000+ over 3 years). A high-BAC (.15+) case, repeat offense, or accident with injury typically pushes total costs to $30,000–$75,000 or more.
A DUI typically remains on your criminal record permanently unless you successfully petition for expungement. For auto insurance rating, most states look back 3–7 years; California looks back 10 years. For employment background checks — especially for jobs requiring a CDL, professional license, or security clearance — a DUI conviction can be a permanent disqualifier in some fields. Some states allow expungement after completing all sentencing requirements; requirements and timelines vary considerably by state, and consulting a licensed attorney about eligibility is advisable once all requirements are complete.
In almost all states, a first-offense DUI results in a license suspension ranging from 90 days to 1 year. Most states offer a hardship or restricted license allowing driving to work, school, and medical appointments during suspension, typically conditioned on installing an ignition interlock device. Some states impose an administrative license suspension through the DMV separate from any court-ordered suspension — both must be resolved to fully restore driving privileges. The specific reinstatement requirements, fees ($150–$500), and timelines vary by state and BAC level at arrest.
DUI (Driving Under the Influence) and DWI (Driving While Intoxicated or Impaired) refer to essentially the same offense — different states simply use different terminology. California, Florida, and Illinois use DUI; Texas and New York use DWI; some states use both depending on substance type. A few states distinguish between alcohol impairment (DWI) and drug impairment (DUI) for charging purposes. The legal penalties, financial consequences, and insurance implications are effectively identical regardless of the acronym used.
DUI attorney fees for a first offense typically range from $1,500–$5,000 for a straightforward plea case, and $5,000–$15,000 or more if the attorney contests the charges through motions, hearings, or trial. Fee ranges vary by state and case complexity. Public defenders are available for those who qualify financially but generally carry heavy caseloads. Most DUI defense attorneys offer free initial consultations — getting 2–3 quotes is standard practice. Given that a conviction can cost $10,000–$25,000+ over 3 years, the attorney fee is typically the most financially sound investment in the case.
