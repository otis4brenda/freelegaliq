About FreeLegalIQ

Legal costs in the United States are notoriously opaque. Unlike buying a car or hiring a contractor, most people have no idea what they're about to spend when they walk into an attorney's office — or even when they're deciding whether to call one at all. Attorney hourly rates range from $150 to over $500 depending on location and specialty. Court filing fees vary by state and case type. Mandatory programs, assessments, and insurance surcharges pile on in ways that nobody explains upfront. The result: millions of Americans either overpay, underprepare, or avoid the legal system entirely because they can't gauge what it will cost them.

FreeLegalIQ was built to fix that. Our free legal cost calculators cover the most common legal situations US consumers face — divorce, DUI, personal injury, estate planning, LLC formation, bankruptcy, child support, speeding tickets, immigration, and small claims court. Each calculator is built on data from bar association surveys, court fee schedules, USCIS fee tables, and insurance industry reports. You enter your specifics — state, income, case type — and get a realistic cost range in seconds. No email required. No sales pitch. No law firm affiliation. Just honest numbers.

We're an independent educational resource, not a lawyer referral service. Our goal is to help you walk into any legal situation — or any attorney's office — with a realistic sense of what you're facing financially. Knowing the cost range in advance lets you budget properly, compare attorney quotes intelligently, and make better decisions about when to hire help versus when to handle something yourself. We update our data regularly to reflect current court fees, attorney rate surveys, and state-specific rule changes.

How These Calculators Work

Each calculator uses a combination of fixed data (court filing fees, mandatory program costs, USCIS fee schedules) and variable formulas that adjust based on your inputs (state, case complexity, income, attorney representation level). For calculations that depend on attorney fees, we use median hourly rate data by state from the most recent ABA and NALP surveys, adjusted for case type and complexity tier.

Insurance impact calculations — used in the DUI, speeding ticket, and personal injury calculators — are based on Insurance Information Institute data and proprietary rate survey data from major insurers operating nationally. Child support estimates use the Income Shares model formula published by state family courts; the Texas and New York percentage-of-income models are handled separately.

Important: These calculators produce estimates, not guarantees. Real-world costs depend on your specific attorney, the complexity of your particular case, local court procedures, and how events unfold. Use these figures as a starting framework for planning and budgeting — not as a final number. For a binding quote, consult a licensed attorney in your state. Many offer free initial consultations.

Among the common legal situations covered here, contested divorces with children and significant assets are typically the most expensive — frequently reaching $50,000–$150,000 per spouse in complex cases. Personal injury litigation that goes to trial can also run into six figures in attorney fees, though most are offset by the contingency arrangement. DUIs are expensive relative to the underlying violation, often totaling $15,000–$25,000 when all costs are included over three years.
Attorneys use different fee structures depending on the case type. Hourly billing is most common for divorce, DUI defense, estate planning, and business formation — you pay for the time spent. Flat fees are common for straightforward matters like simple wills, LLC formation, or uncontested divorces. Contingency fees — where the attorney takes 25–40% of the recovery — are standard for personal injury cases, meaning you pay nothing unless you win. Immigration and bankruptcy attorneys often use flat fees for defined scopes of work.
