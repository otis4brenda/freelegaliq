Child support in the U.S. typically ranges from $400 to $1,200 per month for one child, depending on both parents' incomes, the state's calculation model, and custody arrangement. Forty-one states use the Income Shares model, which considers both parents' combined income; the remaining states use a Percentage of Income model. This calculator provides general estimates based on state guidelines — actual orders are set by a family court judge and vary significantly by case.

How Child Support Is Calculated

In income shares states, the process is: (1) Add both parents' gross (or net, depending on state) monthly incomes; (2) Look up the basic child support obligation for that combined income and number of children in state guidelines tables; (3) Calculate each parent's percentage share of combined income; (4) The paying parent's share of the basic obligation is their base child support payment; (5) Adjustments are made for health insurance costs, childcare, extraordinary medical expenses, and custody time (with significant custody time often reducing the payment).

What Can Modify a Child Support Order?

Child support orders can be modified when there is a "substantial change in circumstances" — typically a change in income of 15–20% or more, a change in custody arrangement, a change in the child's needs, or one parent becoming unemployed. Either parent can petition the court for modification. Many states have automatic review processes every few years. Note: private agreements between parents to pay less than the court order are not enforceable — only a new court order can change the obligation.

Frequently Asked Questions

In the 41 states using the Income Shares model, support is based on both parents' combined gross income and is proportional to each parent's share. For example, if combined monthly income is $6,000 and one parent earns 60% of that amount, they typically contribute 60% of the guideline support figure. The remaining states — including Texas, Wisconsin, and Alaska — use a flat Percentage of Income model based solely on the paying parent's gross income.
The national average child support payment is approximately $500–$650 per month for one child, based on 2026 family law data. This varies widely by state and income level — high-income states like Massachusetts and New Jersey often produce orders of $800–$1,500/month, while rural states may average $300–$500/month for comparable income levels and custody arrangements.
Child support orders can generally be modified when either parent experiences a substantial change in circumstances, such as a significant change in income — most states require a 15–25% shift before the court will consider a modification. Either parent can petition for a review; most states also permit an automatic review every 3 years regardless of income change, particularly when the child receives public assistance.
Custody time typically reduces the noncustodial parent's obligation in most Income Shares states. When a parent has substantial parenting time — generally 40% or more of annual overnights — the base support amount is adjusted downward to reflect the direct costs incurred while the child is in their care. The exact threshold and adjustment formula vary by state.
Child support generally covers basic needs including food, housing, clothing, and school-related expenses. Many court orders also include add-on expenses such as health insurance premiums, unreimbursed medical costs, childcare, and extracurricular activity fees — these are typically split proportionally between parents based on their incomes rather than folded into the base monthly support figure.
