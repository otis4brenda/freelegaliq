The Hidden Insurance Cost of Speeding Tickets

Most drivers focus on the ticket fine and ignore the much larger insurance impact. Here's how it works: after a ticket, insurers classify you as higher risk at your next renewal and raise your premium by 10–30% depending on severity. A minor ticket (1–10mph over) raises rates by roughly 10–15% for 1–3 years. A moderate ticket (11–20mph) raises rates by 15–25% for 3 years. A serious speeding violation (21–30mph) raises rates by 25–40% for 3–5 years. In some states, a 30+ mph over ticket can result in license suspension and create a 50%+ rate increase if the insurer doesn't cancel you outright.

Should You Fight a Speeding Ticket?

For tickets over $300 or in states with heavy point systems, hiring a traffic attorney ($150–$400) is often worth it. Traffic attorneys specialize in getting tickets dismissed, reduced to non-moving violations, or negotiated to keep points off your record. Keeping the ticket off your record saves the insurance increase — which is almost always worth more than the attorney fee. At minimum, consider traffic school (where available) — completing traffic school typically masks the ticket from your insurance company for that renewal cycle in most states.

Frequently Asked Questions

The average speeding ticket base fine in the U.S. ranges from $150 to $500 in 2026, depending on the state and how fast you were going. Court fees, administrative surcharges, and traffic school costs routinely add $100–$300 on top of the base fine, bringing typical first-offense totals to $250–$800 before any insurance impact is factored in.
In most states, a speeding ticket raises auto insurance premiums by approximately 20–30% at your next renewal and typically stays on your record for 3–5 years. For a driver paying $1,500/year in premiums, that's roughly $300–$450 in added annual costs—totaling $900–$2,250 over three years. The insurance impact is usually the largest financial consequence of a speeding conviction.
Penalties vary significantly by state. Virginia is among the strictest—driving 20+ mph over the limit or exceeding 85 mph can be charged as reckless driving, a misdemeanor with fines up to $2,500 plus potential license suspension. Texas, New Jersey, and Nevada also have among the highest combined fine-and-fee totals, while states like Oregon and Idaho have lower baseline fines. School zone and work zone violations typically double the standard fine in most states.
Completing a state-approved defensive driving or traffic school course can reduce or eliminate points on your driving record in most states, often preventing an insurance rate increase. Some states allow ticket dismissal if you complete the course within a set timeframe. An attorney may also negotiate a reduction to a non-moving violation in states like Illinois or New York, which typically has no insurance impact—attorney fees typically run $150–$400 for minor traffic matters.
Whether to fight a speeding ticket depends on the circumstances and your driving record. Contesting in court is generally worthwhile if it was your first offense, the speed was marginally over the limit, or the fine-plus-insurance impact exceeds $1,000. Consulting a local traffic attorney for a free evaluation is a reasonable first step—in many cases they can negotiate a reduced charge or dismissal that saves far more than their fee.

⚖️ When to Hire a Traffic Attorney for a Speeding Ticket

For a typical first minor speeding ticket (1–10 mph over), traffic school is usually the most cost-effective option if you're eligible. A traffic attorney earns their fee when: you were cited for going 21+ mph over the limit; you hold a commercial driver's license (CDL); you're in a state with strict point systems like New York or Virginia where accumulating points quickly leads to license suspension; you have prior violations that make this ticket especially costly; or you were cited in a school zone or construction zone where fines are doubled and insurance impacts are magnified. The break-even calculation is simple: compare the attorney's fee ($150–$500) to the 3-year insurance increase estimate from this calculator — if the insurance cost exceeds the attorney fee, representation pays for itself.
